## Voice 1 — Primary Examiner (§ 101 eligibility + overall)

The claims are directed to a **quantum computing device** (claims 1–11) and a **quantum computing method** (claims 12–18) that operate on **physical nanowires** hosting **non-Abelian quasiparticles (Majorana zero modes)**, driven by **physical gates** that induce phase transitions between topological and non-topological regimes, form **potential wells** in the wire, **trap** a fusion-outcome quasiparticle, and **fuse** it with another Majorana quasiparticle. Claim 5 further recites a **measurement device**. This is quantum hardware being reconfigured to execute a topologically protected computational primitive (qubit fusion) — it is not a bare mathematical concept executed on a generic computer.

**Step 2A Prong One.** Claim 1 recites a nanowire, topological wire segments defining non-Abelian quasiparticles, and gates configured to induce phase changes and potential wells and to fuse quasiparticles. That is a claim to physical apparatus, not to a mental process, a mathematical formula in the abstract, or a bare law of nature. No judicial exception is recited on the face of claim 1. The independent method claim 12 is parallel: it recites steps performed on a nanowire using at least one gate to change phase, trap a fusion outcome, and fuse it with a Majorana quasiparticle. The method claim is tied to specific physical operations on a specific physical substrate — again, no bare judicial exception is recited.

**Claim 10 raises the closest § 101 question** because it recites explicit two-qubit state expressions with normalization denominators. Standing alone, mathematical expressions can be an abstract idea. But claim 10 depends from claim 8, which depends from claim 1, and therefore inherits the entire nanowire + gate + potential-well + fusion apparatus. Under Step 2A Prong Two, the recited state expressions describe the *encoding produced by the physical fusion operation the apparatus performs*; they are not free-floating math. This integrates any exception into a practical application: the operation of a topological quantum processor. Eligible.

**Step 2A Prong Two / Step 2B (across the claim set).** Every independent and dependent claim is anchored in either (a) the physical nanowire hardware and its gates (claims 1–9, 11), (b) a measurement device on that hardware (claim 5), or (c) method steps executed on that hardware (claims 12–18). This is an improvement to the operation of a quantum processor — specifically, a topologically protected primitive (qubit fusion with trapped-well readout) that the specification asserts avoids entangling gates and non-demolitional two-qubit measurements otherwise required to synthesize θ/2-phase gates. That is a concrete technological improvement, squarely on the eligible side of the line.

No claim is directed, in substance, to a bare algorithm or mental process on a generic computer.

## Voice 2 — § 102 Examiner (anticipation / novelty)

The Prior art of record is empty. No reference has been placed of record in this proceeding. Under the examining instructions, I may not cite anticipating references from memory, and the examining instructions expressly direct that in the absence of any reference of record the claims are to be treated as novel under § 102.

No admitted-prior-art anticipation is therefore available on the face of the document.

## Voice 3 — § 103 Examiner (obviousness)

Under Graham and KSR, an obviousness rejection requires (i) the scope and content of the prior art, (ii) differences between the prior art and the claims, (iii) the level of ordinary skill, and (iv) any objective indicia — with a specific, articulated rationale to combine references. Here the prior-art-of-record list is empty. Under the examining instructions, no § 103 rejection can be sustained on unlisted references or references recalled from memory.

I note for the record that the specification's Background admits that MZM-based topological qubits, braiding for Clifford gates, magic-state distillation, and the general π/8-phase-gate paradigm were known. But the claimed combination requires *specifically* a gate configured to induce a potential well that traps the fusion outcome of qubit-A / qubit-B quasiparticle fusion and to subsequently fuse that trapped outcome with a second quasiparticle of one of those qubits. That specific "trap-then-fuse" primitive is characterized by the disclosure as new. Even taking the admitted prior art at face value, no combination of the admitted prior art alone teaches or suggests the trap-then-fuse limitation. Absent record references to the contrary, no obviousness combination survives.

## Voice 4 — § 112 Examiner (enablement / written description / definiteness)

The written description IS included in this record, so § 112(a) is properly assessed.

**§ 112(a) — Written description.** The specification describes at length: the standard MZM encoding (four quasiparticles per qubit, fusion trees for |0⟩ and |1⟩), the physical realization on semiconductor/s-wave-superconductor heterostructures with the topological condition |V_z| > √(μ² + Δ₀²), formation of a potential well by a kink in the chemical potential, adiabatic well migration ("playing the scale on the piano"), local energy readout distinguishing I from ψ by ~10 meV, and a specific phase-gate protocol (FIGS. 9A–9E). The claimed elements — nanowire, topological/nontopological segments, gate(s) inducing phase changes and a potential well, trapping of the fusion outcome, and fusion with a second quasiparticle — are each supported. Possession of the invention as claimed is shown.

**§ 112(a) — Enablement.** Applying the Wands factors: the disclosure identifies the physical system (Majorana nanowire on s-wave superconductor), the control parameters (V_z, μ, Δ₀) and the means to vary them (electrical/magnetic gating), the readout mechanism (local energy measurement of the ~10 meV ψ excitation), and worked-out fusion algebra including F-moves and bubble contractions. A PHOSITA in the topological-quantum-computing art, given the specification, is enabled to make and use the claimed device and to perform the claimed method within the disclosed scope. I note that experimental realization of Majorana modes is technically demanding, but the claim scope is properly bounded to the disclosed heterostructure architecture and does not reach an unenabled functional breadth (no "with high fidelity", no "fault-tolerantly", no unbounded speedup limitations appear in the claims).

**§ 112(b) — Definiteness.** I have several concrete concerns.

1. **Claim 3** recites "the potential well such that the potential well and *the second end of the first topological wire segment* are brought towards each other." Claim 1 does not previously establish "a second end of the first topological wire segment." This is an antecedent-basis defect. (The specification supports the concept — endpoints of topological segments are described — but the claim itself does not introduce the endpoint before referring to "the second end.")

2. **Claim 4** recites "wherein *at least one gate* is further configured to cause the potential well to brought towards the first topological wire segment." Two defects: (a) claim 1 already establishes "at least one gate," so claim 4 should recite "the at least one gate" for proper antecedent basis; (b) "to brought" is a grammatical error rendering the limitation unclear — the intent is plainly "to be brought," but as issued the phrase is indefinite on its face.

3. **Claim 14** recites "the second end of the first topological wire segment" — the same antecedent-basis defect as claim 3, in the method claim.

4. **Claim 15** recites "wherein at least one gate is further configured to cause the potential well to brought towards the first topological wire segment" — same "at least one gate" antecedent question and same "to brought" grammatical defect as claim 4. Additionally, claim 15 is a method claim of claim 12 but recites a *configuration* of a gate rather than a method step; the mixing of apparatus-style "configured to" language into a method claim creates a further definiteness concern about what act the method step actually requires.

5. **Claim 8** recites "a first encoding" and "a second encoding" without defining the term "encoding" or providing objective bounds. In view of the specification's use of "standard encoding" and the explicit dependent claim 10 (which pins down the encoding via state expressions), "encoding" is understood in the art and by the specification. This is not a definiteness defect.

6. **Claim 10** recites state formulas including a normalization denominator. The formulas are mathematically well-defined and the term N_xy is defined in the claim itself as the fermion parity of the fusion outcome. Not indefinite.

7. **Claim 9** recites "the encoding of the first qubit or the second qubit is selected so that the produced qubit corresponds to application of a phase gate on a computational qubit." The claim omits the introductory "wherein," but that is a formalism, not indefiniteness. The functional recitation is supported by the phase-gate protocol of FIGS. 9A–9E.

8. **§ 112(f) means-plus-function.** Claim 1's "at least one gate configured to selectively induce…" and "fuse the trapped non-Abelian fusion quasiparticle…" uses "configured to" with a definite structural nonce ("gate"). "Gate" in the specification denotes an electrostatic or magnetic gate — a concrete physical structure. § 112(f) is not invoked. Claim 5's "measurement device for measuring…" is closer to means-plus-function form ("device for X-ing"), but "measurement device" is a term of art (the specification specifically identifies an interferometer), providing sufficient structure. § 112(f) treatment is unlikely, and even if invoked, the specification discloses corresponding structure (FIG. 4, "measuring device 118 such as an interferometer").

## Voice 5 — Quantum Technical Specialist (operability / quantum-specific)

The claimed invention is grounded in the physics of one-dimensional topological superconductors and Ising/Majorana non-Abelian anyons. The physics is real and well-posed:

- The topological/trivial phase boundary for a proximitized semiconductor nanowire is correctly stated as |V_z| = √(μ² + Δ₀²), consistent with the standard Oreg–Lutchyn model. Adiabatic tuning of V_z, μ, or Δ₀ can drive segments across the boundary and extend/join topological regions. **Operable.**
- Fusion of two σ (Ising) anyons has fusion rule σ × σ = I ⊕ ψ. Trapping the ψ outcome in a chemical-potential well and reading it out via local energy measurement (ψ sits ~10 meV above the ground state) is a physically sound proposal. **Operable, subject to real-world calibration.**
- Adiabatic transport of the well and subsequent tunneling/fusion with a Majorana endpoint is a standard adiabatic-manipulation technique in the topological-QC literature. **Operable.**
- The θ/2-phase-gate protocol (FIGS. 9A–9E) with the |B_θ⟩ ancilla and the derived state expressions (claim 10) is mathematically consistent with the F-move algebra shown in FIG. 6 and correctly accounts for the two possible fermion-parity outcomes N_xy ∈ {0, 1} with a Clifford correction. **Operable and mathematically checked.**

The claims do NOT assert prohibited or physically overreaching results. There are no claims to:
- fault-tolerance thresholds without a fault-tolerant construction;
- decoherence-time bounds;
- complexity-class speedups;
- cloning of unknown states;
- unbounded fidelity or scalability.

The scope is bounded to the physical operation of trap-and-fuse for topological qubit fusion on Majorana wires. **No § 101 "law of nature" overreach.** **No § 112(a) inoperability defect.**

One physics-facing caveat for the record: experimental realization of unambiguous MZMs in nanowires remains an ongoing subject of scientific dispute in the field. That, however, is a question of whether the *underlying substrate* has been demonstrated, not whether the claimed *device architecture and method* are operable given a Majorana wire. The specification correctly conditions its Majorana-wire disclosure on the topological-phase inequality and the disclosed gating. A PHOSITA can build and operate the claimed device to the extent Majorana wires can be realized, which is exactly what enablement requires — not a guarantee that Majorana wires are trivial to fabricate.

**Technical verdict: the claims describe a real, operable topological-quantum-computing primitive.**

## Voice 6 — Supervisory Patent Examiner (SPE) — synthesis and disposition

I have reviewed the five preceding voices.

- Voice 1 (§ 101) allows every claim: hardware-anchored, integrated into a practical application, no bare abstract idea.
- Voice 2 (§ 102) allows every claim: no reference of record; instructions forbid recall-based rejections.
- Voice 3 (§ 103) allows every claim: no combination of record; admitted prior art alone does not reach the "trap-then-fuse" limitation.
- Voice 4 (§ 112) identifies concrete § 112(b) defects at claims 3, 4, 14, and 15 (antecedent basis and, in 4/15, a grammatical error and method/apparatus mixing). All other claims pass § 112.
- Voice 5 (Quantum Technical Specialist) confirms operability and finds no § 101/§ 112(a) overreach.

The § 112(b) defects at claims 3, 4, 14, and 15 are real and non-trivial: "the second end" lacks antecedent basis in claims 3 and 14; "to brought" in claims 4 and 15 is on its face ungrammatical to the point of indefiniteness; and claim 15 improperly imports apparatus-configuration language into a method claim. Under USPTO practice a definiteness rejection on these grounds must issue. Because those rejections stand against issued claims of record, the correct disposition is **non-final-rejection**, not allowance.

Claims 1, 2, 5–13, and 16–18 contain allowable subject matter — they are novel and non-obvious over the prior art of record, satisfy § 101, and have no § 112 defect. Claims 3, 4, 14, and 15 contain allowable subject matter that would be placed in condition for allowance by curing the antecedent-basis, grammatical, and claim-category defects identified above.

The single strongest issue the applicant must overcome is the § 112(b) cluster at claims 4 and 15 ("to brought"). The § 112(b) defects at claims 3 and 14 (antecedent basis for "the second end") are secondary but equally required.

`Disposition: non-final-rejection`

**Claims standing rejected:** 3, 4, 14, 15 under § 112(b).
**Claims with allowable subject matter as-is:** 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 16, 17, 18.
**Claims with allowable subject matter contingent on amendment:** 3, 4, 14, 15.

**Amendments that would place the application in condition for allowance:**
1. Amend claim 1 (and correspondingly claim 12) to introduce "a first end and a second end" of the first topological wire segment, so claims 3 and 14 have proper antecedent basis.
2. Amend claim 4 to: "wherein the at least one gate is further configured to cause the potential well to be brought towards the first topological wire segment."
3. Rewrite claim 15 as an active method step: "further comprising causing, with the at least one gate, the potential well to be brought towards the first topological wire segment."
